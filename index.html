About

Hi! I’m Emma, a mixed-methods social science researcher. My work bridges HCI, Psychology, Social Work, and Health to understand how technologies shape people’s in-person experiences in ways that could induce harm, and how research practices can better support the safety of those who have experiences of trauma through application of the trauma-informed approach.

When I'm not doing research, I do ceramics, hike, and lift heavy weights.

Projects

Trauma-Informed Data Donation

Research on designing data donation processes that support participant safety, agency, and wellbeing when people share potentially sensitive personal data.

Understanding Online Behaviors that Precede In-Person Sex

Research examining how people interpret online dating behaviors as signals of sexual openness and how these interpretations may shape subsequent in-person experiences, consent, and wellbeing.

Quantitative Research Understanding Women's Fear of Rape

My psychology research examines how women's perceptions of sexual violence and fear of rape relate to vigilance and behaviors intended to protect against sexual victimization.

This work draws on my training in experimental psychology, quantitative research design, and statistical analysis to understand how perceived risk shapes behavior.

For instance, in one study, I examine women's perceived risk of rape by perpetrator type for themselves, a female friend, and a member of the general population.
