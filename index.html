About

Hi! I’m Emma Walquist, a mixed-methods social science researcher interested in mitigating technology-facilitated in-person harm and protecting participants who share data about harmful experiences. My work bridges HCI, Psychology, Social Work, and Health to understand how technologies shape people’s experiences and how research practices can better support their safety. My dissertation is the first step in my broader research program, which examines how dating apps shape in-person sexual experiences, including sexual violence. Specifically, I investigate how users interpret online behaviors as signals of sexual openness and how these interpretations may influence subsequent in-person interactions. Alongside this work, I develop methodological recommendations for conducting data donation studies with participants sharing data related to potentially traumatic experiences. My approach to research is grounded in my training in experimental quantitative psychology, where I developed a strong foundation in statistics, research design, and academic writing for psychology audiences during my master’s degree. After completing my MS in experimental psychology, I transitioned to HCI to connect empirical research with the design of technologies, research practices, and policies that promote people’s safety and well-being.
